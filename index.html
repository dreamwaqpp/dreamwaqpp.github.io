Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal Locomotion with Resilient Multi-Modal Reinforcement Learning

I Made Aswin Nahrendra1, Byeongho Yu1, Minho Oh1, Dongkyu Lee1, Seunghyun Lee1, Hyeonwoo Lee1, Hyungtae Lim2, Hyun Myung1
1Urban Robotics Lab, KAIST, 2SPARK Lab, MIT
Paper arXiv Video

DreamWaQ++ enables agile locomotion over diverse obstacles: (a) ascending and (b) descending stairs, (c) leaping, (d) probing uncertain terrain, (e) crossing gaps, (f) adapting to deformable terrain, (g) balancing on moving platforms, and (h) climbing 35° slopes—all in a single neural network.

Abstract

Quadrupedal robots hold promising potential for navigating cluttered environments with resilience akin to their animal counterparts. However, their floating base configuration makes them vulnerable to real-world uncertainties.

Deep reinforcement learning has become a plausible alternative for robust locomotion control. However, approaches relying solely on proprioception require front-feet contact to detect obstacles—sacrificing collision-free locomotion. Meanwhile, incorporating exteroception typically requires precisely modeled terrain maps.

We propose a novel method to fuse proprioception and exteroception with resilient multi-modal reinforcement learning. The result is an agile controller that traverses rough terrains, steep slopes, and high-rise stairs while remaining robust against out-of-distribution situations.

🦿 The Limitation of Blind Locomotion

Blind controllers rely only on proprioception—joint positions, velocities, and IMU data. They're remarkably robust but have a fundamental limitation: they can only sense obstacles after collision. Watch what happens when a blind controller approaches stairs:

💡 The Key Insight

Animals don't walk blindly—they look ahead to plan their footsteps. DreamWaQ++ gives quadrupeds this same ability by fusing proprioception with 3D point cloud perception.

Method Overview

A hierarchical perception-control framework with spatio-temporal mixing

DreamWaQ++ comprises a perception pipeline and a control pipeline. The encoder has a hierarchical structure with low-level measurement encoders and a spatio-temporal mixer for efficient multi-modal fusion.

The controller receives two streams of information: proprioception (joint states, body orientation) and exteroception (3D point clouds from a depth camera). These are encoded separately, then fused through a spatio-temporal mixer that captures both spatial terrain features and temporal dynamics.

Exteroceptive encoder architecture for processing 3D point clouds.

The learned latent space captures meaningful terrain properties.

🔍 Emergent Probing Behavior

One of the most fascinating outcomes is the emergent probing behavior. When faced with uncertain terrain (like a hidden gap), DreamWaQ++ cautiously extends its front leg to probe the surface before committing—just like animals do!

Movie S4: Emergent probing behavior when navigating uncertain terrain.

Analysis of the probing behavior: the robot hesitates and probes before stepping onto uncertain terrain.

🧠 Why does this emerge?

This behavior wasn't explicitly programmed—it emerged from the multi-modal fusion. When visual information is ambiguous, the controller becomes more cautious and relies on proprioceptive feedback to verify terrain properties.
