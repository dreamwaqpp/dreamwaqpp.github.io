DreamWaQ++

Obstacle-Aware Quadrupedal Locomotion with Resilient Multi-Modal Reinforcement Learning

1KAIST · 2KRAFTON · 3URobotics · 4MIT

Abstract

Quadrupedal robots hold promising potential for applications in navigating cluttered environments with resilience akin to their animal counterparts. However, their floating-base configuration makes them susceptible to real-world uncertainties, presenting substantial challenges in locomotion control.

Deep reinforcement learning has emerged as a viable alternative for developing robust locomotion controllers. However, approaches relying solely on proprioception often sacrifice collision-free locomotion, as they require front-foot contact to detect stairs and adapt the gait. Meanwhile, incorporating exteroception necessitates a precisely modeled map observed by exteroceptive sensors over time.

This work proposes a novel method for fusing proprioception and exteroception through a resilient multi-modal reinforcement learning framework. The proposed method yields a controller demonstrating agile locomotion across diverse real-world courses, including rough terrains, steep slopes, and high-rise stairs, while maintaining robustness in out-of-distribution situations.

Method Overview

Overview of DreamWaQ++: a hierarchical perception-control framework with proprioceptive and exteroceptive encoders fused through a spatio-temporal multi-modal mixer.

PointNet-based exteroceptive encoder with a confidence filter layer that rejects unreliable point features before aggregation.

Learned latent representations capture terrain properties, enabling the controller to distinguish different terrain types.

Obstacle Awareness

DreamWaQ++ demonstrates affordance-aware locomotion by adapting its foot swing trajectory to overcome obstacles. The controller extends its swing phase for combined rises up to 30 cm and retains memory of the terrain structure beneath the robot, enabling it to adapt in real time even on asymmetric stair configurations.

Movie 3: Obstacle negotiation across various stair configurations.

Affordance-aware locomotion on asymmetric stairs with real-time foot swing adaptation.

Quantitative comparison of obstacle climbing success rates across different stair rise/run configurations. DreamWaQ++ achieves 20–40% higher success rates than visual baselines.

Emergent Probing Behavior

When facing uncertain terrain, DreamWaQ++ develops an emergent probing skill—the robot deliberately stops and probes the surface with its front legs before committing to a step. This cautious behavior arises naturally from training without explicit supervision, demonstrating the controller's ability to handle uncertainty adaptively.

Movie 4: Emergent probing behavior on uncertain terrain.

Analysis of the probing behavior: velocity profiles, estimation error, and knee flexion angles showing deliberate terrain checking before committing.

Out-of-Distribution Robustness

DreamWaQ++ gracefully handles out-of-distribution situations never encountered during training, including sudden changes of foothold, sensor degradation, unexpected obstacles, and novel terrain types. When rapidly removed from a moving platform, the controller enlarges its support polygon by 20% for a safe landing. The multi-modal latent context forms distinct clusters reflecting real-time adaptation.

Movie 5: Adaptation in out-of-distribution situations.

Response to sudden foothold changes: velocity profiles, support polygon expansion, and 2D embedding visualization showing distinct adaptation clusters.

Extreme Slopes

DreamWaQ++ successfully navigates slopes up to 35 degrees—far beyond the 10-degree maximum seen during training. The controller autonomously adopts a crawling gait with reduced body height, resulting in rear leg torques 1.5× lower than the blind baseline DreamWaQ.

Movie 6: Performance on extreme slopes up to 35°.

Torque comparison between DreamWaQ++ and DreamWaQ on steep slopes, showing significantly lower rear leg torques with the crawling gait.

Large Obstacles

DreamWaQ++ overcomes obstacles larger than the robot's leg length through emergent parkour-like behaviors: the Unitree Go1 develops a jumping motion for 0.6 m obstacles, ANYmal-C uses a climbing motion for 1.0 m obstacles, and Hound employs a swinging motion for 1.5 m obstacles. In real-world tests, Go1 with a 2.5 kg payload successfully climbed a 41 cm soft sofa.

Movie 8: Overcoming large obstacles with emergent leaping.

Large obstacle climbing on Go1 (0.6 m), ANYmal-C (1.0 m), Hound (1.5 m), and real-world deployment with payload.

Ablation Studies

Embedding Analysis

Proprioceptive context forms an ellipsoidal pattern reflecting foot dynamics, while exteroceptive context shows clearer inter-class terrain separation. The fused multi-modal context displays distinct terrain-based clusters, with the proprioceptive pattern persisting to correct unreliable exteroceptive data.

PacMAP visualization of proprioceptive, exteroceptive, and multi-modal context embeddings across different terrain types.

Component Analysis

Removing the latent fusion mechanism causes the largest performance drop (60.7% on hard stairs). Memory and contrastive loss are critical for collision avoidance, while the versatility gain objective promotes exploration and diverse gait discovery.

Cross-modal feature correlation analysis showing proprioceptive-exteroceptive alignment across terrains.

Terrain reconstruction from latent features, compared against ground truth.

Latent Modulation for Gait Control

Specific exteroceptive embedding features directly control gait properties. Scaling up identified latent features decreases gait frequency while increasing step height, producing gaits that resemble stair-climbing behavior.

Latent modulation: scaling exteroceptive embeddings directly modulates gait frequency and step height.

Resilience under exteroception failures: foot-trapping reflex under noise input, and stable recovery when camera is detached.
